# Design System: 丹青词境

## 1. Visual Theme & Atmosphere

The interface should feel like a contemporary literati studio rather than a generic AI dashboard. The atmosphere is calm, tactile, and editorial: warm paper tones, ink-dark typography, restrained accents, and broad breathing room that lets each action feel intentional. The design balances three identities at once: a writing desk, an atelier wall, and a lightweight control console. It avoids neon futurism and avoids museum-like austerity; instead it uses soft translucency, brush-wash gradients, and lacquer-red accents to suggest craft, scholarship, and authorship.

The visual rhythm is built on contrast between quiet surfaces and precise highlights. Large rounded panels read like mounted sheets of paper or carved trays. Accent moments use cinnabar red and muted gold, never as flat branding blocks but as small seals, active chips, and emphasis strokes. The main emotional target is composed confidence: the product should feel elegant enough for poetry and painting, but operationally clear enough for repeated workflow use.

## 2. Color Palette & Roles

- Ink Black Brown (#16110F): Primary reading color for headings and dense interface text.
- Burnt Lacquer Red (#8B3F1F): Primary action color for major calls to action and selected emphasis.
- Bright Lacquer Copper (#BE6032): Secondary gradient stop for action buttons and active highlights.
- Scholar Jade (#315954): Cool counterweight used for secondary actions and subtle technical contrast.
- Muted Gold Leaf (#C9A063): Warm signal tone used for chips, small markers, and ornamental highlights.
- Rice Paper Cream (#F8F3EB): Main background for large surfaces and content panels.
- Warm Parchment (#EFE5D7): Structural background behind the interface grid.
- Tea-Stained Border (#BBA791): Soft perimeter and dividers where structure is needed without heaviness.

## 3. Typography Rules

- Headlines and cultural emphasis use Noto Serif SC for gravity, cadence, and literary character.
- Operational UI, controls, metadata, and dense reading use Outfit for clarity and speed.
- Large titles are compact and vertical in feeling, with low line-height and generous spacing around them.
- Supporting copy should remain soft and readable, generally using a line-height around 1.65 to 1.85.

## 4. Component Stylings

- Buttons: Pill-shaped, lightly elevated, with primary actions in lacquer red gradients and secondary actions on pale paper backgrounds.
- Cards and containers: Generously rounded corners, translucent paper surfaces, whisper-soft shadows, and subtle highlight overlays.
- Preset cards: Compact selection tiles with mild state contrast; selected state uses warm tinted paper instead of saturated fills.
- Inputs and textareas: Smooth rounded forms on quiet paper backgrounds, with visible but restrained focus rings.
- History items: Thumbnail- or excerpt-led blocks that keep actions close without collapsing the content hierarchy.

## 5. Layout Principles

- The page is split into three narrative zones: orientation, composition, and result.
- A sticky left rail provides mode switching and short guidance so the main work area stays focused, but the overall page never exceeds a two-column reading pattern.
- The central workbench is modular, built from ordered numbered cards stacked in a single main column so the experience feels calmer and more ceremonial than dashboard-like.
- The result panel should prioritize presentation first, interpretation second, and history third, and should sit within the same primary column instead of forming a third competing lane.
- Spacing should remain generous, especially around section starts and between control clusters, to avoid the claustrophobic feel common in tool-heavy interfaces.
